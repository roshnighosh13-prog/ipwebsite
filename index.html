Section I · Practice Areas

Representation Across Protection, Branding & Contracts

Each engagement is structured around clarity of ownership and enforceable documentation. Expand any practice area below to examine the statutory scope of work and the records required to initiate an instruction.

Scope of Legal Work

  • Music & Sound Recordings: Distinct registrations for lyrics (Literary Works), musical scores and compositions (Musical Works), and recorded masters (Sound Recordings).
  • Visual & Fine Arts: Protection of paintings, illustrations, album artwork, graphic identity systems, and photographs as Artistic Works under Section 2(c).
  • Literature & Software: Registration of books, manuscripts, screenplays, and software code (source and object code) as Literary Works under Section 2(o).
  • Film & Animation: Registration of live-action films and animated productions as Cinematograph Films under Section 2(f), alongside underlying character and concept art.
  • Advisory on commissioned work defaults under Section 17, author moral rights under Section 57, and formal replies to Copyright Office discrepancy notices.

Required Client Documentation

  • Copies of the Work: Two copies in the prescribed format (audio files or notation for music; high-resolution plates for visual art and photography; manuscripts or redacted code extracts for literature and software; video files or storyboards for film and animation).
  • Creator Particulars: Full legal names, residential or business addresses, and nationalities of all authors, composers, artists, writers, programmers, or producers.
  • Publication Record: Year and country of first publication where the work has already been released, exhibited, published, or deployed publicly.
  • Clearances & Authorization: Written No Objection Certificates (NOCs) from co-creators or commissioning parties where applicable, together with a signed Power of Attorney.

Scope of Legal Work

  • Pre-Filing Clearance: Phonetic and device searches across the IP India database to assess registrability and identify conflicting prior marks prior to public brand launch.
  • Application & Prosecution: Filing and prosecution of word and device mark applications with nationwide protection across India.
  • Nice Classification Strategy: Tailored class coverage across Class 9 (software, apps, games, recorded media), Class 16 (books, zines, fine art prints), Class 25 (apparel and branded merchandise), Class 28 (collectibles), Class 41 (live performances, film and animation production, photography, publishing), and Class 42 (SaaS and software engineering).
  • Examination Replies: Drafting reasoned legal responses to Examination Reports raising objections under Sections 9 and 11 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999.

Required Client Documentation

  • Mark Specification: Exact word mark (stage name, studio identity, imprint, or product title) and high-resolution artwork where a logo or stylized device is claimed.
  • Prior Use Evidence: Date of first commercial use in India supported by dated invoices, streaming profiles, exhibition catalogues, or app store listings, or instructions to file on a “Proposed to be Used” basis.
  • Applicant & Fee Status: Legal particulars of the individual creator, partnership, or corporate entity, alongside Udyam / MSME registration where eligible for reduced statutory filing fees.
  • Authorization: Executed Form TM-48 (Authorization of Agent / Power of Attorney).

Scope of Legal Work by Discipline

  • Music & Audio: Co-writer split sheets, producer agreements, session performer releases, sync licenses, IPRS royalty carve-outs, and label or distribution contracts.
  • Visual & Fine Arts: Commission agreements for painters and illustrators, commercial versus editorial photography licenses, gallery consignment agreements, and model or property releases.
  • Literature & Software: Book publishing and translation agreements, screenwriter option contracts, independent contractor IP assignment deeds, SaaS terms, and open-source compliance audits.
  • Film & Animation: Cinematographer and animator engagement agreements, character IP licensing, post-production vendor contracts, and festival or OTT distribution agreements.

Required Client Documentation

  • For Contract Reviews: The complete draft agreement received from a client, label, studio, publisher, or agency, accompanied by a concise summary of the commercial terms negotiated.
  • For Bespoke Drafting: Full legal names of all contracting parties, agreed ownership or royalty percentages, deliverables, fee schedule, permitted scope of use, duration, and territory.
  • Commercial Correspondence: Any prior term sheets, statements of work (SOW), or written correspondence recording the commercial understanding between the parties.
Section II · Collective Rights & Music Royalties

The Indian Performing Right Society (IPRS)

A complete guide for lyricists, composers, singer-songwriters, music publishers, and production houses on how IPRS operates under Section 33 of the Copyright Act, 1957, how songwriter royalties differ from distributor payouts, and how our chambers assist with IPRS registration readiness.

Statutory Mandate · Section 33, Copyright Act, 1957

What IPRS Is & What Royalties It Collects

The Indian Performing Right Society Limited (IPRS) is India’s sole registered copyright society under Section 33 of the Copyright Act, 1957 authorized to license and collect royalties for musical works (melody and composition) and literary works associated with musical works (lyrics).

Under the 2012 Amendment to the Copyright Act (Sections 18 and 19), authors (lyricists) and composers hold a statutory, inalienable right to receive an equal share of royalties alongside the publisher or assignee whenever their musical and literary works are utilized in any form (outside public exhibition in a cinema hall). Furthermore, under Section 19(8), any private contract that purports to assign rights contrary to the terms already assigned to a registered copyright society of which the author is a member is void.

Public Performance Royalties

Collected from live concerts, ticketed events, broadcast television, FM and satellite radio, hotels, restaurants, retail spaces, and commercial establishments playing music publicly.

Mechanical & Digital Royalties

Collected from audio and video streaming platforms (Spotify, Apple Music, JioSaavn, YouTube, OTT platforms) and physical or digital reproductions of underlying compositions and lyrics.

Critical Distinction Two Royalty Streams

Why Releasing Through a Distributor Does Not Register Your Song with IPRS

Stream A · Sound Recording (Master)

Digital distributors and aggregators (such as DistroKid, TuneCore, Believe, or record labels) collect revenue strictly for the recorded audio file and pay the master owner.

Stream B · Lyrics & Composition (IPRS)

IPRS collects performing and mechanical royalties for the underlying lyrics and melody, distributing royalties quarterly in India and internationally via reciprocal societies (PRS, ASCAP, BMI, APRA, GEMA) through your unique IPI Number.

Membership Structure, Official Fees & Documentation

Who Can Join IPRS & What Is Required

Typical processing timeline: ~45 working days

1. Categories & Official Fees

  • Author & Composer Members (Individuals) Open to lyricists, composers (music directors), singer-songwriters, and legal heirs of deceased writers. Official Fee: ₹1,200 (One-Time)
  • Owner Publisher Members (Entities / Labels) Open to music publishers, record labels, and film or series producers holding publishing rights in musical and literary works. Official Fee: ₹2,200 (₹3,400 Dual Role)
  • Note: Statutory fees are paid directly online on the official IPRS portal. IPRS does not authorize third-party commission agents.

2. Eligibility & Enrolment Stages

  1. Mandatory Released Work: Applicants must have at least one commercially released or published work with verifiable credits (YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music, JioSaavn, or physical CD inlay). Unreleased demos do not qualify.
  2. Online Application & Work Notification: Submission of personal or entity profile, bank details, identity records, and Work Notification details for each released track.
  3. Execution of Statutory Deeds: Upon initial scrutiny, IPRS dispatches physical copies of the Deed of Assignment and Indemnity Bond, which must be executed and returned within 15 days.
  4. IPI Allocation & Catalogue Upload: On receipt of the signed deeds, IPRS issues your unique IPI Number, grants provisional membership (ratified by the Board and AGM), and enables bulk work and cue-sheet uploads.

3. Required Documentation

  • PAN Card (Mandatory): The applicant’s name on the application, bank account, and song credits must align cleanly to prevent verification delays.
  • Identity & Address Proof: Aadhaar card, Passport, Voter ID, Driving Licence, or registered Leave & Licence agreement.
  • Bank Account Verification: Cancelled cheque leaf, passbook front page, or bank statement displaying account holder name, account number, and IFSC code.
  • Photograph & Foreign NOC: Recent front-facing colour passport photograph, plus an NOC if already registered with a foreign society (such as PRS, ASCAP, or BMI).
  • Work & Entity Proofs: Streaming links or screenshots showing writer and composer credits (or production house letters and cue sheets for film, OTT, or advertising works), plus incorporation or partnership and GST records for publisher entities.

Section III · Pre-Release Legal Audit

Before You Release, Publish, or Deploy

Select your creative or technical discipline below to review four foundational legal checkpoints under Indian law prior to launching a project or executing a contract.

01 · Underlying Works vs. Master Recording

Separate Composition Rights from the Sound Recording

Under the Copyright Act, 1957, a single recorded track contains three distinct legal assets: lyrics (literary work), musical score (musical work), and recorded audio (sound recording). Confirm written ownership across all three layers before uploading to a distributor.

02 · Co-Writer Split Sheets & IPRS Readiness

Execute Split Sheets & Register Works with IPRS

Record songwriting percentages and master revenue shares in a signed split sheet at the close of studio sessions. Once released, register your membership and work notifications with IPRS so performing and mechanical royalties are collected alongside distributor master payouts.

03 · Stage Name & Moniker Clearance

Clear Your Artist or Band Name Across Core Classes

Before investing in branded merchandise or releasing a catalogue under a stage moniker, conduct a clearance search on the IP India registry across Class 9 (recorded media), Class 25 (apparel), and Class 41 (live performance and music production).

04 · Label & Distribution Agreements

Distinguish Fixed-Term Licenses from Assignments

Examine whether a proposed contract grants a time-bound distribution license or transfers permanent copyright ownership, and verify how recoupable advances, statutory author royalty shares under Section 18, and catalogue reversion clauses operate.

01 · Section 17(b) Commissioned Defaults

Override Statutory Ownership Defaults in Commissions

Under Section 17(b) of the Copyright Act, 1957, when a photograph is taken or a painting or portrait is created for valuable consideration at a client’s instance, the client becomes the first owner of copyright unless an explicit written contract states otherwise.

02 · Physical Object vs. Reproduction Rights

Separate Physical Artwork Sales from Usage Licenses

Selling an original canvas or delivering high-resolution image files does not transfer copyright. Define in writing whether the buyer acquires display rights only, a bounded commercial usage license (by medium, term, and territory), or a full assignment.

03 · Section 57 Moral Rights

Safeguard Attribution and Integrity Across Media

Even where economic copyright is licensed or assigned, painters, illustrators, and photographers retain statutory moral rights under Section 57 to claim authorship and restrain unauthorized cropping, distortion, or modification prejudicial to their reputation.

04 · Releases & Source Deliverables

Secure Model Releases and Scope Editable Source Files

For commercial shoots and brand campaigns, obtain signed model and property releases, define included revision rounds, and license editable layered design files or merchandise reproduction under a separate commercial fee tier.

01 · Section 2(o) Literary Classification

Protect Manuscripts and Source Code as Literary Works

Under Section 2(o) of the Copyright Act, 1957, books, screenplays, and computer programs (source code, object code, and databases) are protected as Literary Works. Because computer programs per se are excluded from patentability under Section 3(k) of the Patents Act, 1970, copyright and contracts carry primary legal weight.

02 · Employment vs. Independent Contractor IP

Audit Freelance Code and Ghostwriting Assignments

Works created under a contract of employment vest in the employer under Section 17(c), whereas code or writing produced by an independent contractor remains owned by the creator unless transferred by a written assignment specifying duration and territory under Section 19.

03 · Subsidiary Rights & Open-Source Dependencies

Carve Out Adaptations and Audit Third-Party Packages

Authors should expressly retain or separately value translation, audiobook, and screen adaptation rights in publishing agreements. Software developers should audit third-party open-source libraries (copyleft GPL versus permissive MIT or Apache licenses) before commercial deployment.

04 · Product, SaaS & Imprint Trademarks

Clear Application Names, SaaS Brands & Series Titles

Copyright protects concrete expression rather than standalone titles or underlying functional concepts. Conduct trademark clearance searches across Classes 9 and 42 (for software and SaaS products) or Classes 16 and 41 (for publications and imprints) prior to launch.

01 · Cinematograph Film vs. Underlying Art

Separate Film Rights from Character and Concept Art

Under Section 2(f) of the Copyright Act, 1957, live-action motion pictures and animated sequences are protected as Cinematograph Films, while character sheets, storyboards, background paintings, and 3D models qualify independently as Artistic Works under Section 2(c).

02 · Producer, Cinematographer & Animator Terms

Define Crew Ownership, Showreel & Project File Rights

Under Section 2(d)(v), the producer is recognized as the statutory author of a cinematograph film. Cinematographers and animators should record written terms governing portfolio showreel usage, custody of raw footage or working files, and reuse of proprietary rigs or shaders.

03 · Chain-of-Title Documentation

Compile Clearances Ahead of Festival & OTT Delivery

Prior to submitting a film or animated work to festivals, broadcasters, or streaming platforms, assemble signed NOCs and synchronization or master licenses covering music cues, IPRS cue sheets, stock assets, underlying scripts, voiceover talent, and location releases.

04 · Character & Production Banner Marks

Register Original Animated Characters & Studio Names

Where original animated properties or production studios contemplate merchandise, games, or derivative series, register studio names and distinctive character names or logos across Classes 9, 25, 28, and 41 on the IP India registry.
